\chapter*{Abstract}\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Fashion ecommerce requires the same size and colour identity to remain consistent from product discovery to warehouse picking and post-purchase service. This report specifies Atelier Fashion, a fictional direct-to-consumer retailer transitioning from disconnected catalogue, order, and inventory records to an integrated information system. The analysis establishes business activities, stakeholders, information needs, functional and nonfunctional requirements, priorities, and business rules. UML models describe actor goals, workflow responsibilities, transaction lifecycles, and domain relationships. The proposed design separates product designs from sellable variants, temporary carts from inventory reservations, and order fulfilment from payment and refund status. It uses immutable order snapshots, atomic stock allocation, authenticated provider notifications, and explicit return inspection to address overselling and inconsistent financial records. The report defines acceptance scenarios and requirement traceability for later implementation. It is a design study, not an implemented or performance-tested production system.
